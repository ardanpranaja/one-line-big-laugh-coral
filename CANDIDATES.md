# Candidate one-liners

Competition: One Line, Big Laugh.
Constraint: a single funny line, at most 200 characters, that lands entirely
on its own — no title, setup or explanation around it. The judge weighs, in
order: a real joke rather than a quirky observation, a turn the reader did
not see coming, tight writing, freshness, and staying in the head after one
reading.

## Candidates

1. (133 chars) I told my therapist I feel invisible. She said she'd check my
   file — honestly, that's the most attention anyone has paid me all year.

2. (145 chars) I asked the AI for a joke about my love life and it said,
   'Error: no data found,' which is the most accurate thing anyone has ever
   said about me.

3. (142 chars) My smart fridge emailed me about my eating habits, so I
   unplugged it — now it's the only thing in this house with a cold shoulder
   and a point.

4. (117 chars) I finally found a diet that works: I just read the terms and
   conditions out loud until my appetite files for divorce.

5. (133 chars) I told my calendar I needed a break and it blocked out the
   whole week — turns out even my schedule is better at boundaries than I am.

6. (96 chars) My Roomba ran away from home, which is impressive for
   something whose entire job is coming back.

7. (122 chars) I bought a self-help book called 'How to Stop Overthinking'
   and now I can't stop wondering whether I bought the right one.

8. (137 chars) My plants died, my goldfish moved out, and my Wi-Fi left me
   for the neighbors — but at least my phone still lies to me about the
   battery.

## Pick

Candidate 1. It opens with a real human setup (a therapy session about
feeling invisible), then turns on the one person paid to listen — the
therapist brushes the patient off by checking a file — and the closer flips
the neglect into a punchline: being ignored by the therapist counts as the
most attention the patient has had all year. Every word earns its place, the
joke needs no setup outside the line, and the final clause is the part someone
repeats to a friend later. The AI, fridge, Roomba and calendar lines lean on
tired tech-as-roommate templates; the diet and self-help lines telegraph their
punchline from the first words. Candidate 1 is the only one where the turn
genuinely surprises.
